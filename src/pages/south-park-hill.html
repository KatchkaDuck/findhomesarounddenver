Life Here

Parkways, Porches and City Park Next Door

A Planned Suburb in the City

Park Hill got its name in 1887, when Baron Alois von Winckler platted land just east of City Park. Early rules required 40-foot setbacks and homes far more expensive than the Denver average, which is why the streets feel so wide and green today. Denver annexed the area in 1903.

The Streets

Montview Boulevard is the neighborhood's grand street, lined with towering trees and big older homes. Smaller pockets of shops and restaurants sit along Kearney Street, 23rd Avenue and Colfax.

City Park Next Door

City Park, Denver's largest park, borders the neighborhood to the west. It holds the Denver Zoo, the Denver Museum of Nature & Science and a golf course.

Getting Around

Downtown is an easy commute, and quiet streets with bike lanes make cycling popular. Being on the east side also makes it one of the quicker central neighborhoods to drive to Denver International Airport.

Questions Buyers Ask

South Park Hill FAQ

Where Is South Park Hill?

South Park Hill is on Denver's east side, bounded roughly by Colorado Boulevard on the west, 23rd Avenue on the north, Quebec Street on the east and Colfax Avenue on the south. City Park is just to the west.

How Did Park Hill Get Its Name?

In 1887, Baron Alois von Winckler platted about 32 blocks just east of City Park and called the development Park Hill. Denver annexed the area in 1903.

Is South Park Hill a Good Place to Live?

For buyers who want classic homes, tree-lined streets and City Park nearby, yes. It's also convenient to the airport. The trade-offs are older homes and a longer drive to south Denver and the Tech Center.

Which Schools Serve South Park Hill?

Park Hill School, a Denver landmark, is a neighborhood elementary school, and East High School sits just west by City Park. Boundaries vary by address, so confirm a specific home's assigned schools with Denver Public Schools.
