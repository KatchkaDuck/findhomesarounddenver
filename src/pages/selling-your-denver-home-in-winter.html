Should You Sell Your Denver Home This Winter?

If you can wait, I'd usually list after the holidays. If you need to sell now, winter has more going for it than most people think.

By Chris Nowakiwsky, Broker Associate, RE/MAX ProfessionalsNovember 2, 2026

For most Denver homeowners, I'd recommend waiting until after the holidays to list. Mortgage rates are still above 7%, buyer sentiment is wavering a bit, and there are more homes for sale than we've seen in years. But plenty of people don't get to choose their timing. If you need to sell in the next couple of months, there are real positives to listing now, and a few things you can do to make the most of them.

Rates are a full point higher than a year ago, when Freddie Mac's average was 6.17%. That stretches every buyer's budget, and it shows. Buyers are taking their time, comparing options and looking for deals. Homes are still selling close to their list price, but the ones that sell are priced right from the start.

Why I'd Usually Wait Until After the Holidays

Between Thanksgiving and New Year's, a lot of buyers step away. Schedules fill up, people travel, and anyone on the fence about a 7% rate finds it easy to put the search off until January. If you have no deadline, listing into that lull means more days on market, and in a market with this much inventory, a listing that sits starts to look like a deal waiting to happen.

If you have the flexibility, use the next two months to prepare instead: repairs, inspections, photos and a pricing plan, so you're ready to go when buyers come back.

If You Need to Sell, Winter Is a Market of Need on Both Sides

Here's how I've been describing this time of year to clients: the homes on the market right now mostly belong to people who need to sell, not people testing the waters. The same is true of the buyers. Someone touring homes in December, with rates above 7%, usually has a reason to move: a new job, a growing family, a lease that's ending.

That works in your favor. You'll see fewer casual lookers and more buyers who are ready to write an offer. Conversations are more direct, and both sides tend to want the same thing: a fair deal and a firm closing date.

It also means buyers will expect to negotiate. The best way to stay in control is to get ahead of that, with the right price on day one and a clear idea of what you're willing to offer.

The Upsides of Listing in the Next Couple of Months

  • Less competition. DMAR notes that active listings typically taper through the fourth quarter and some sellers pull their homes for the holidays. Its read on the season: buyers who stay in the market "will face less competition than at any other time of year." That also means more attention for each home that stays listed.
  • Serious buyers. Fewer showings, but more of them turn into offers.
  • A head start on spring. Last winter, new listings jumped more than 150% from December to January, according to DMAR. A home that's already on the market, or under contract, before that wave doesn't have to compete with it.
  • Rates are easing a little. The 30-year average fell to its lowest level of the month in late October. Freddie Mac said that's "modestly boosting purchasing power for prospective buyers." Every small drop brings a few more buyers off the sidelines.
  • Incentives that speak to today's buyer. With rates this high, a seller credit toward a rate buydown or closing costs can do more for a buyer's monthly payment than the same amount off the price. It's worth talking through before you list.

How to Prepare a Home for a Winter Listing

Winter adds a few Colorado-specific items to the usual seller prep.

  • Keep walks and steps clear. Denver expects homeowners to clear sidewalks by the day after a snow, and a slick front step is the first thing a buyer notices. Arrange snow removal in advance if you'll be away.
  • Get photos while the weather is good. If there's a clear, dry week in November, take exterior photos then. A sunny exterior shot does a lot of work in a snowy month.
  • Test for radon before you list. The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment says about half of Colorado homes are above the EPA action level of 4.0 pCi/L. Tests are run with windows and doors closed, which is easy in winter, and knowing your number ahead of time takes a common inspection surprise off the table.
  • Scope the sewer line. Many older homes in neighborhoods like Platt Park and Washington Park still have clay sewer lines. A pre-listing sewer scope lets you price in any repairs instead of negotiating them later.
  • Service the furnace and gather records. Buyers will be in the house when the heat is running. A recent service receipt and a warm, quiet house build confidence. Many inspectors won't run an air conditioner in freezing weather, so keep any AC service records handy too.
  • Make it feel warm. Turn the heat up a degree or two before showings, turn on the lights, and keep a spot by the door for boots.

Price for the Market You're In

Winter buyers have watched the market all fall and know which homes have been sitting. The listings that sell in December and January are the ones priced for today's rates and today's inventory, not last spring's. A price cut later tells buyers your original number was wrong, and they negotiate from there.

Whether to list now or wait usually comes down to your timeline, your home and what's selling on your street right now. That's worth a short conversation before you decide.

Common Questions

Is Winter a Bad Time to Sell a House in Denver?

Not if you need to sell. Fewer buyers are looking, but there are also fewer homes for sale, and the buyers who are out tend to be motivated. If you have no deadline, though, I'd usually wait until after the holidays while rates are above 7%.

Should I Take My Listing Off the Market for the Holidays?

Some sellers do, and that thins out the competition for everyone who stays. If you need to sell, your home is priced right and you can handle a few showings in December, staying on the market can work in your favor. If you don't need to sell, a holiday break and a fresh start in January is a reasonable choice.
