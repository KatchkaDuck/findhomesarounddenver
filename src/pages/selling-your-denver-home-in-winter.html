Should You Sell Your Denver Home This Winter?

For a lot of sellers, yes. Winter brings fewer showings, but it also brings less competition and more serious buyers. The question is whether your home and your timeline fit the season.

By Chris Nowakiwsky, Broker Associate, RE/MAX ProfessionalsNovember 2, 2026

Winter can be a good time to sell a house in Denver, especially this year. Fewer homes come on the market between Thanksgiving and early January, so a well-priced listing faces less competition, and the buyers still looking tend to be the ones who need to move. The trade-off is fewer buyers overall and a market that rewards correct pricing more than ever.

What the Numbers Say About Winter

The Denver metro market has a predictable rhythm. In its most recent report, the Denver Metro Association of Realtors (DMAR) noted that active listings typically taper through the fourth quarter and that some sellers pull their homes for the holidays. Its read on the season: buyers who stay in the market "will face less competition than at any other time of year."

That last pair is the one I'd pay attention to. New listings more than doubled from December to January last winter, and homes in the $750,000 to $999,999 range took longer to sell once that wave arrived. A listing that goes live in December is competing with far fewer homes than the same listing in mid-January.

Last winter was also warmer than usual, and DMAR's committee said the whole market "leapt into action early." If that repeats, the window of light competition could be shorter than the old "wait until after the Super Bowl" rule suggests.

When a Winter Listing Makes Sense

  • You need to move on a timeline. A new job, a growing family or a home you've already bought won't wait for April. Winter buyers are often on the same kind of clock.
  • Your home shows well in winter. Good natural light, a mudroom, a fireplace, a south-facing driveway that melts quickly. Homes that feel warm and easy in January stand out.
  • You want to get ahead of spring. This fall's inventory is the highest in years. If even a share of that returns in spring along with the usual new listings, you'll have more competition then, not less.
  • You're buying your next home too. Selling first in a quieter market lets you shop with a firm budget, and winter buyers have more room to negotiate. Our sell and buy guide walks through the order of operations.

When It Might Be Better to Wait

  • Your home sells on its yard. Gardens, mature trees and big lawns show best in May. If that's the main draw, spring photos are worth the wait.
  • You're not ready. Rushing a listing before repairs and prep are done costs more than a few months of waiting.
  • You have no reason to move. In this market, anyone selling needs a good reason to. If you don't have one, staying put is a fine choice.

How to Prepare a Home for a Winter Listing

Winter adds a few Colorado-specific items to the usual seller prep.

  • Keep walks and steps clear. Denver expects homeowners to clear sidewalks by the day after a snow, and a slick front step is the first thing a buyer notices. Arrange snow removal in advance if you'll be away.
  • Get photos while the weather is good. If there's a clear, dry week in November, take exterior photos then. A sunny exterior shot does a lot of work in a snowy month.
  • Test for radon before you list. The Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment says about half of Colorado homes are above the EPA action level of 4.0 pCi/L. Tests are run with windows and doors closed, which is easy in winter, and knowing your number ahead of time takes a common inspection surprise off the table.
  • Scope the sewer line. Many older homes in neighborhoods like Platt Park and Washington Park still have clay sewer lines. A pre-listing sewer scope lets you price in any repairs instead of negotiating them later.
  • Service the furnace and gather records. Buyers will be in the house when the heat is running. A recent service receipt and a warm, quiet house build confidence. Many inspectors won't run an air conditioner in freezing weather, so keep any AC service records handy too.
  • Make it feel warm. Turn the heat up a degree or two before showings, turn on the lights, and keep a spot by the door for boots.

Price for the Market You're In

Winter buyers are fewer, and most of them are serious. They've also watched the market all fall and know which homes have been sitting. The listings that sell in December and January are the ones priced right on day one. A price cut in February tells buyers your original number was wrong, just as spring competition arrives.

If you're not sure whether now or spring is the better fit, it often comes down to your home, your next move and what's selling on your street right now. That's worth a short conversation before you decide.

Common Questions

Is Winter a Bad Time to Sell a House in Denver?

Not necessarily. Fewer buyers are looking, but there are also fewer homes for sale, and DMAR notes that buyers who stay active late in the year face less competition than at any other time. Well-priced, well-prepared homes still sell in winter.

Should I Take My Listing Off the Market for the Holidays?

Some sellers do, and that thins out the competition for everyone who stays. If your home is priced right and you can handle a few showings in December, staying on the market can work in your favor.

When Does the Spring Market Start in Denver?

Traditionally after the Super Bowl in February, but last winter new listings jumped more than 150% from December to January, and DMAR's committee said the market started early. Listing before that wave gives you the most room.
