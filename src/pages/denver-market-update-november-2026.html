Denver Real Estate Market Update: November 2026

Prices held steady in October, but fewer homes sold and most sellers ended up helping with the buyer's costs, which gives buyers real room to negotiate right now.

By Chris Nowakiwsky, Broker Associate, RE/MAX ProfessionalsNovember 9, 2026

Everyone that is dipping their toe in the market right now is doing so for a reason. Buyers are looking for opportunity, and sellers have a compelling reason to sell.

Even with inventory levels rising, sellers have been holding firm on their prices. The median single-family home in the metro sold for $645,000 in October, about the same as last year, while condos and townhomes sold for a median of $375,000, down about 3%.

The bigger change is how many homes are actually selling. Closings were down 23.5% from last October and pending sales were down 20%, so I expect December to be slow as well. Inventory dipped from September, which is normal heading into the holidays, but it's still higher than a year ago, and condos and townhomes now have more than seven months of supply.

What I'm Seeing on the Ground

Homes are still selling for about 98% of list price, but that number doesn't include concessions. Almost two out of three sellers in October paid something toward the buyer's closing costs or a rate buydown, and the average was $12,557.

In central Denver, I'm seeing more price reductions and more homes to choose from, and buyers are taking their time and looking for deals. With the average 30-year rate at 7.18% in early November (Freddie Mac), most buyers are focused on their monthly payment, so a rate buydown can often help them more than a small price cut.

Anyone selling right now needs a good reason to, and the sellers I see doing well are the ones who priced right from the start and knew what they were willing to give before the first showing.

The $1 Million-Plus Market

Homes over $1 million are still selling faster than the rest of the market. In October they sold in a median of 17 days, compared with 30 for the metro overall and 34 for $1 million-plus homes a year ago. They closed at about 98% of list price, and sales were down about 24% from last year, in line with the rest of the market.

Supply is about the same from $750,000 up to $2 million, at roughly four months for single-family homes, but above $2 million it jumps to almost eight months, and that's where buyers have the most leverage. In neighborhoods like Washington Park and Bonnie Brae, a well-priced home under $2 million is still selling.

What It Means for Sellers

  • Expect to offer a concession. Most buyers are asking for help with closing costs or a rate buydown, so plan for it when you set your price.
  • Compare a buydown to a price cut. The same dollars put toward a buyer's rate can lower their payment more than taking it off the price, and I'm happy to run both numbers for you.
  • Price for today. With pending sales down 20%, an overpriced home is likely to sit through the holidays, because your competition is what's for sale now, not what sold last spring.
  • If you can wait, get ready. Use the next two months for repairs, inspections and photos so you can list when buyers come back in January.

What It Means for Buyers

  • Ask for a concession. Most sellers are already giving one, so asking for help with closing costs or a rate buydown is a normal request right now.
  • Shop through the holidays. Fewer people tour homes between Thanksgiving and New Year's, and sellers still on the market usually need to move.
  • Look at homes that have been sitting. Condos and townhomes, and homes over $2 million, have the most supply and the most room to negotiate.
